Privacy and device data

The current version of Itemity has no server operated by 852 Technologies LLC and no advertising service. Your inventory, photos, settings and optional device-local login profile (display name, username, a hashed password, an authenticator secret, a hashed date of birth used only to recover the authenticator, an optional recovery email address and an optional profile picture) are stored on your device, not in an online account with us. Some data is processed by third-party services only as described below. The database that holds your records (items, purchases, usage, categories, locations, people and settings) is encrypted using a random key that the App creates on your device and keeps in its secure storage. Photos and icons are stored as ordinary files in the App’s private storage and are not encrypted by the App. If you add a home-screen widget, a short summary of your inventory (for example the names of items that are expiring, your shopping list and reorder suggestions) is kept unencrypted in the App’s private storage so the widget can show it; it is visible on your home screen whenever the widget is, even if the App’s login is turned on.

When you request a barcode lookup, the barcode number is sent to the third-party lookup services described above. Like other internet requests, those services also receive technical request data such as your IP address and may process it under their own privacy policies. No other inventory fields are intentionally included in the lookup request. When you choose a 3D icon that isn't built into the App, it is downloaded once from a public hosting service for an open-source icon library, which receives technical request data such as your IP address. When a lookup finds a product photo, the App may download it from the address the service gave, which can be a server of Open Food Facts or of a retailer or other website listed by UPCitemdb; that server receives technical request data such as your IP address.

To sell and restore Itemity Pro features, the App uses Google Play or Apple App Store billing and an independent purchase-management provider. The App sends that provider a random identifier created for this installation of the App (not your name or email address), purchase and restore records, and technical data such as the app version, platform, store country and ordinary network request data (such as your IP address). The provider and the store process this data under their own privacy terms to verify purchases and tell the App which features you own. Your inventory data is not sent.

The App uses an independent app-update and diagnostics provider to deliver app updates and to measure performance. When the App starts, it checks the provider’s servers for updates, sending technical data such as the app version, platform and a random identifier created for this installation. The App also sends the provider anonymous performance and crash reports: start-up and screen-drawing times, device model and operating-system version, battery, temperature and network state, a summary of the network requests made during start-up (such as which servers were contacted), and details of errors and crashes, tagged with that random installation identifier. These reports do not include your inventory, photos or login details, and are used only to find and fix problems. The provider processes this data under its own privacy terms.

The App accesses the camera or photo library only when you choose a feature that needs it, such as scanning a barcode or adding a photo, and subject to your device permissions. Photos remain device-local unless you choose to export, share or back them up.

When you create or share a backup, you choose the destination. The recipient or storage provider you select—including a cloud-storage, file or email provider—may receive the backup and related technical data under its own terms and privacy policy.

If you choose to send a problem report, your email app opens with the report so that you can review it before sending. It contains technical information—the app version, device model and operating system, app language, this installation’s support ID, app settings, counts of stored records, and recent error details (including the screens and actions just before the newest error)—but not item names, photos or login details.

If you contact us, we receive the contact details and message content you provide. We may use and retain them as reasonably necessary to respond, provide support, protect legal rights, prevent abuse and comply with law. We do not sell personal information.

Because we do not possess your device-local App data, you normally access, correct or delete it using the App or your device, including by clearing App data or uninstalling. Purchase records held by our purchase-management provider can be deleted on request; please include the support ID shown in About > Application Info. Applicable law may give you additional rights (see “Your privacy rights” below). For privacy questions or requests concerning information we may hold, contact support@852technologies.com

Your data and backups

You are solely responsible for maintaining suitable backups and for checking that they can be restored. Uninstalling the App, clearing its data, device failure, loss, theft, operating-system changes or App errors may permanently erase data that is not recoverable from a valid backup.

The App keeps your inventory only on your device. Android's automatic backup and cloud-backup services do not include it, so uninstalling the App, clearing its data or switching to a new phone leaves you with no data. To keep your data, create a backup in the App (More > Backup / Restore) and save it outside the App, for example in cloud storage. The App can remind you to back up regularly.

Backup files are intended to be encrypted using a password you choose. We do not receive or store that password and cannot recover it. A forgotten password, damaged file, incompatible version or technical failure may make a backup unusable. Files you export as CSV (More > Import / Export) are not encrypted: anyone who can open the file can read it.

You are responsible for protecting your device, login details, backup passwords and exported files, and for the security and availability of any location or service where you store or send a backup.
